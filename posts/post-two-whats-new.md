## ICE & Minneapolis

Seems like everyone has read the headlines by now. Things in Minneapolis are tense. But beyond the fear/anxiety/conflict, there is a massive community that has largely come together in support of each other. One manifestation of this are the massive protests you all have been seeing. Awareness being spread on social media. But there are many other manifestations of this that are invisible to those not on the ground here: the makerspace that I work in 3D printing safety whistles for the community around the clock. Students/community members distributing them. Posters all over the place either protesting ICE or educating about how to deal with ICE. Lots of newly locked doors for protection. There’s a massive community effort to educate people about the rights that all people who stand on US soil have. A sort of unspoken (and spoken) understanding exists between many individuals now. I wonder if this is the same sort of thing that was felt by communities in past times of turmoil. A mentor and friend of mine David tells me about his daily experience nowadays: ICE camped outside his family’s grocery store. Waiting for the bus to campus while an ICE officer stares at him from across the street. Three times so far, there have been ICE protests across the street from my apartment building. The purpose of these protests have been to make noise (apparently there were ICE agents staying in the hotel there). Students have brought all manner of noise making devices to these protests. Presumably to make the noise reach both the ICE agents and the people allowing them to sleep on campus. I haven’t attended any protests. So, the noise has helped me sleep at night. It’s been mostly washed away now, but somebody spray painted the phrase “MURDERERS SLEEP HERE” on the wall. 

All of this makes me think of two great 1971 albums: *What’s Going On* by Marvin Gaye and *There’s a Riot Goin’ On* by Sly & the Family Stone. 

## What I’ve Been Up To

I’ve been starting a few new things recently and gaining some new interests. Mainly surrounding entrepreneurship and AI. A couple months ago I started to build a service business where I help content creators monetize their platform. It’s been going pretty good; I have two clients right now and they are great to work with. However, my focus has mostly shifted in the past couple weeks. A little over a week ago, I caught wind of something called OpenClaw. Basically what OpenClaw does is create a gateway on your computer that connects AI to the controls of the computer, a messaging app, and the internet. What this creates is a very useful tool that is able to do nearly anything a human would be able to do by using a computer. This creates a lot of opportunity but also a lot of security risks. I have been experimenting with OpenClaw using the AI makerspace computers I have access to on campus which has been interesting. Some things I am trying to make the AI do for me is build a social media app for a class project and control a DAW (digital audio workstation) so it can make sounds that I use in music. 

Another place I have been putting my time is in building another business called Morpho. The idea behind it is that I will automate marketing small businesses and early stage founders. I’ve been using an AI service called Claude Code (by the company Anthropic) to build it. I just launched the site and if you are interested you can check it out here:

## Real AI Hype.  Real AI Danger.

I say the AI hype is real not because AI is capable of what the AI boosters say it will be capable of. I say the hype is real because AI will do what the AI boosters say it will, if we let it. AI is far from conscious. AI also is not actually that "intelligent". However, if we allow tech oligarchs to  use their influence, money, and hype machine to force "AI" into peoples lives, it will happen. If we let it, "AI" will replace entry level jobs and take over various tasks in peoples lives. But, it will do it poorly. Actually it will do it averagely (which in my mind is poorly). The way "AI" is being developed right now optimizes for the most average result. Large Language Models (LLMs), which is the type of "AI" that is stirring up the most hype, essentially calculates the most probable output for the input it receives based on the data set it was trained on. These data sets are massive and basically use the entire Internet. So, you can imagine why the results would be average. Furthermore, because of this, the results are often inaccurate and or made up. What AI does do is make many tasks much easier. So what we are seeing happening is people using AI to take over easily done tasks, but the tasks are being done badly (or not uniquely). I think this is pretty sad. I don't think we should allow for the results of our efforts to become much worse simply because it has become much easier to create the results (using AI). I put "AI" in quotes because it doesn't really mean much. At its core "AI" is a marketing term. "AI" is only useful as a term for those trying to sell their product much more advanced thing than it really is. 

I say AI is dangerous because automation and AI has already proven to be dangerous. And if not dangerous, unhelpful. I've been reading the book *The AI Con* by Emily M. Bender & Alex Hanna. In the book they describe myriad ways that automation has hurt people and ways that AI has and might hurt people. To me though, the real danger is in letting AI and algorithmic automation further penetrate social services, the judicial system, government, and the healthcare system. This already has gone poorly and created unjust results in these systems. Not to mention the massive impacts that AI is set to have on the environment along with the impacts it's already having on communities around the United States. I won't go into examples in depth, but if you are curious read the book I mentioned above and watch the videos about the impacts of AI data centers on local communities that I link in the Go Deeper section of this post. 

A great quote from The AI Con: 
> “the allure and prestige of AI raise the risk of narrowing fields of inquiry to those questions which can be approached with these tools.”

This all may make it seem like I am anti-AI. That is not the case. As you have learned, I am an avid user of AI. My point is this: If you want to, use LLMs and other types of new AI tech. But use them knowing their capabilities, limitations, and impacts. Use them for what they are good at (not much) and don't let them take over important parts of our lives (like we are seeing people do). 
Here is what I think AI is good for: prototyping and volume. If you are prototyping and need to understand what something *might* look like, AI is great (notice this use case does not demand accuracy or excellence). If you want to do a lot of volume of a monotonous task, AI is great. Keep in mind that AI will produce results that are inaccurate, impossible, or not useful and it requires a lot of human input to turn AI output into something good.
